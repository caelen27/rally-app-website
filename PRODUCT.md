# Rally

register: brand

## What it is

A mobile platform that connects youth aged 15 to 25 across the Greater Toronto
Area with local charities, non-profits, and recreational leagues. Participants
find volunteer shifts and roster spots, log hours that their school will accept,
and get matched to openings by a strengths survey plus travel distance.

This repository is the marketing landing page, not the app.

## Users

**Participants (15 to 25).** Most arrive because they need community involvement
hours to graduate; Ontario requires 40. They are phone-first, sceptical of
anything that feels like a school assignment, and will bounce off a page that
talks down to them. A meaningful share are minors, and anyone under 16 needs a
guardian to approve the account.

**Organizations.** Small charities, food banks, shelters, rec leagues. Usually
one overworked coordinator with no recruiting budget. They need to trust the
platform with young people before they will post anything, and they are the ones
who pay: $10 a month to list, plus 10 percent of any performance bonus they
choose to award through Rally.

The page has to hold both audiences at once. Energetic enough that a
sixteen-year-old does not close the tab, sober enough that a charity board is
willing to sign up.

## Tone

Direct and unfussy. Short sentences. Concrete nouns: shifts, hours, roster
spots, transit distance. Say the actual number rather than gesturing at
outcomes.

Never: youth-marketing slang, hustle language, or charity-sector uplift about
giving back and making a difference. No invented statistics, ever. The only
figure on the page is the real 40-hour Ontario graduation requirement.

## Anti-references

- Volunteer boards that look like municipal government intranets.
- Gig-economy apps. Rally is not selling shifts, and Participants are not
  contractors.
- Charity sites built on stock photography of smiling strangers.
- Generic startup landing pages: gradient hero, three icon cards, testimonial
  wall, pricing table.

## Principles

1. **Say what happens.** "Check in when you arrive and the coordinator confirms
   it" beats "streamlined hour tracking".
2. **Never overstate the hours.** Rally records and exports; school boards
   decide. That caveat appears on the page, in the FAQ, and in the terms.
3. **Safety is the product's real risk.** Copy never implies Rally screens or
   supervises anyone. It does not.
4. **Free for youth stays visible.** Any pricing surface says who pays.
5. **The matcher suggests, the person chooses.** No language that implies the
   algorithm decides anything.

## Constraints

- Greater Toronto Area at launch: Toronto, Peel, York, Durham, Halton.
- Ontario and Canadian law: PIPEDA, AODA, CASL, Consumer Protection Act 2002.
- No testimonials until real ones exist. Fabricated reviews are not an option.
